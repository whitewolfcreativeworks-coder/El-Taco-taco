# El Taco Taco — Project Packet 001
**Status:** Exploratory brainstorm / owner discovery pending  
**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Purpose:** Shared starting point for owner conversations and collaborating development agents. Nothing in this packet is an approved business agreement or build authorization.

## 1. Project vision
Help the family-owned El Taco Taco food truck business in Eugene–Springfield, Oregon strengthen its digital presence as it prepares to open a second location in the Gateway area. Build an attractive, authentic website and integrated digital commerce operation that can support both locations, direct pickup, delivery, loyalty, and ongoing promotion. Preserve the family's identity and make their food the centerpiece.

## 2. Current understanding — confirm with owners
- Existing business has strong food and local customer interest.
- Existing website and Facebook presence need improvement; DoorDash currently provides third-party delivery.
- Second location planned in Gateway area; exact address, opening date, menus, hours and operations unverified.
- Owner expressed interest in meeting with the project lead and his wife to discuss ideas.
- No commitments, ownership transfers, permissions, budgets, or final terms have been agreed.

## 3. Proposed platform
### Customer
- Mobile-first website; downloadable app or installable PWA as an option.
- Authentic photos, short videos, story of the family, two locations, menus and hours.
- Online ordering for pickup, delivery, and possible advance/catering orders.
- Location selection, payment, order status, SMS/push notifications, loyalty points and promotions.
### Restaurant
- Two-location order dashboard: accept/reject orders, prep estimates, menu availability, hours, specials, delivery handoff.
- Easy nontechnical editing of everyday information; avoid code deployments for routine menu changes.
### Drivers
- Separate driver workflow: available orders, acceptance/assignment, navigation, status updates, proof of delivery where appropriate.
- Driver pay, tips, insurance and employment/contractor arrangement are separate from the technology partner's 2% proposal.
### Marketing
- Facebook, Instagram, Google Business Profiles and appropriate local channels; evaluate X and Nextdoor.
- Scheduled AI-assisted promotional calendar with owner-approved content and platform-compliant publishing.
- Track attribution, promotion performance, customer retention and Gateway opening campaign.

## 4. Commercial proposal — discussion only
- Alternative A: owners pay a mutually agreed development fee and own the resulting agreed deliverables.
- Alternative B: no upfront development charge; project lead receives a proposed **2% of completed food sales processed through the new website/app ordering system**, whether pickup or delivery, including advance orders.
- Exclude ordinary walk-up/register sales and unrelated DoorDash orders.
- Proposed commission basis excludes taxes, tips, delivery charges, cancellations, refunds and chargebacks.
- Discuss ongoing maintenance obligations, hosting/payment/SMS/maps/marketing costs, reporting, payout mechanics, customer data ownership, software licensing, exit/buyout and duration. Do not assume perpetual terms without a negotiated written agreement.
- The 2% does not compensate drivers; delivery labor economics must stand independently.
- Before payment processing, verify applicable contracts, tax treatment, privacy requirements and payment-provider constraints.

## 5. Delivery concepts — unapproved
- Initial minimum food order concept: $15, or qualifying bundled packages.
- Explore distance-based service zones, perhaps a compact 3–5 mile pilot and larger zones with higher minimums; 15–20 miles only when economics support it.
- Location-aware dispatch and customer notifications.
- Project lead may personally perform initial deliveries to validate workflow, subject to suitable insurance and operating arrangements.
- Model food preparation time, travel time, driver compensation, tips, delivery fee, failed deliveries and food quality before setting zones.
- Do not build a compensation model that relies on tips to substitute for legally required pay.

## 6. Loyalty and promotion ideas — experiments, not policy
- One taco purchased = one taco point; ten points redeem for one qualifying free taco.
- '11 tacos for the price of 10' bundle, with rewards applied without double counting.
- Exploratory idea: additional taco points tied to delivery tipping; assess economics, fairness, payment-provider terms and any legal restrictions before use. Keep driver tips entirely with drivers as applicable.
- Alternative: delivery bonus points independent of tips.
- Daily specials, office lunches, referral promotions, Gateway opening campaign and seasonal offers.
- Calculate reward costs and redemption limits before launch.

## 7. Owner discovery and content intake
Create a dedicated project Gmail address after choosing an approved name. Ask the owners to send:
- Their vision, priorities, ideas and what they dislike about current digital presence.
- Existing website/social/profile links, logos, brand colors, menus, prices, specials and food descriptions.
- Photos/videos they own and authorize for use; stories, biographies and correct spelling of names.
- Both addresses, opening date, hours, contact methods, pickup practices and delivery constraints.
- Current POS, DoorDash/other ordering relationships, payment processor and any existing loyalty tools.
- Preferred languages, customer service expectations, accessibility needs, and who approves content.
- Permissions for filming, identifiable staff/customer appearances, and social account access through proper role-based invitations (never email passwords).

### On-site content plan
Photograph exterior/truck, both locations, menu items, ingredients, preparation, packaging, team portraits and family story. Film short vertical clips of cooking, plating, customer handoff and new-location preparation. Obtain permission/releases for recognizable people and avoid recording private customer information. Keep original assets organized, with owner approval and usage rights recorded.

### Suggested intake folders (do not store sensitive data in a public repo)
- Brand and logos
- Menu and pricing
- Food photography
- Kitchen and family video
- Locations and hours
- Marketing ideas and approvals
- Business/technical integrations

## 8. Multi-agent working rules
- Treat this document as an idea register, not a finalized scope.
- Label every statement as verified, owner-provided, proposed, or unresolved.
- No autonomous deployment, public posting, spending, payment onboarding or account access without explicit approval.
- Use feature branches/PRs and document assumptions, tests, and changes.
- Never commit customer data, payment credentials, API keys, unpublished private images, or confidential business documents.
- Prefer private asset storage and only publish licensed/approved derivatives.
- Design for portable data and a clean exit path for the business.

## 9. Open questions for the owners
1. What does success at the Gateway opening look like?
2. Which online ordering and POS tools are in use today?
3. What can the kitchen handle at peak times at each location?
4. What menus, prices, hours and delivery areas should differ by location?
5. Who will operate dispatch and delivery, and how will drivers be compensated?
6. Who owns/controls current domain and social profiles?
7. What marketing channels do they want to use, and who approves promotions?
8. What level of ongoing support do they want?
9. Would they prefer a paid build or a digital-sales revenue share?
10. What permissions and budget are available for content, hosting, messaging and advertising?
